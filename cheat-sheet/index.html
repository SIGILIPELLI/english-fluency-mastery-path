    English Fluency & IELTS Cheat Sheet¶

    Quick-reference tables from 30 lessons of the free English Fluency & IELTS Mastery Path, in course order from beginner to advanced. Each section links to the full lesson with explanations and runnable examples.

    Level 1 · Entry¶

    08 · Introduction to IELTS¶

    Fact Detail
    Total core testing time Listening + Reading + Writing ≈ 2 hours 40 minutes, done in one sitting
    Speaking Separate, 11-14 minutes, same day or different day
    Score validity IELTS scores are typically accepted as valid evidence of proficiency for 2 years by receiving institutions
    Retake policy You can retake IELTS as many times as needed; scores are per-attempt, past scores don't affect new ones
    Format options Paper-based and computer-delivered versions exist; content and scoring criteria are the same either way

    09 · IELTS Speaking Part 1 Basics¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I answer the actual question first? Examiners notice if you dodge the direct question
    Is my answer 2-4 sentences, not 1 and not a full talk? Matches Part 1's conversational pace
    Did I add a reason ("because...")? Shows Lexical Resource and Grammatical Range
    Did I add one small extra detail or comparison? Makes the answer feel natural, not rehearsed
    Did I speak without long silent pauses? Directly scored under Fluency and Coherence

    Level 2 · Intermediate¶

    03 · IELTS Speaking Part 2¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I use all 60 seconds to plan, in short phrases only? Full sentences waste time and lead to reading, not speaking
    Did I address every bullet point on the card? Directly supports Coherence — shows a complete, organized answer
    Did I speak for close to the full 1-2 minutes? Running under a minute is read as a fluency weakness
    Did I add at least one specific, concrete detail? Vague answers score lower on Lexical Resource
    Did I finish with a proper closing sentence? Avoids sounding like you simply ran out of things to say

    04 · IELTS Speaking Part 3¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I give a clear position, not just "maybe, I don't know"? Vague non-answers cap your Fluency and Coherence score
    Did I support my opinion with a reason? Shows Grammatical Range through complex, reasoned sentences
    Did I include at least one concrete example? Distinguishes strong answers from generic ones
    Did I use hedging language appropriately ("tends to," "arguably")? Natural for abstract topics — shows nuanced Lexical Resource
    Did I avoid memorized "template" phrases that don't fit the question? Examiners are trained to spot rehearsed, irrelevant answers

    05 · IELTS Writing Task 1 Basics¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I write an overview sentence with no specific numbers? Directly required for Task Achievement
    Did I group data logically (by category, by time period)? Supports Coherence and Cohesion
    Did I use a range of trend vocabulary, not just "increased" repeatedly? Raises Lexical Resource
    Did I stay descriptive and avoid giving reasons/opinions? Task 1 only asks you to report, not explain or judge
    Is my response at least 150 words? Under-length responses are penalized on Task Achievement

    06 · IELTS Writing Task 2 Basics¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I correctly identify the question type (section 1)? Answering the wrong task type caps Task Achievement
    Does my introduction state a clear position? Examiners look for this specifically
    Does each body paragraph cover exactly one main idea (PEEL)? Central to Coherence and Cohesion scoring
    Did I include at least one specific example per body paragraph? Generic, example-free paragraphs score lower
    Is my essay 250+ words, with a distinct conclusion? Under-length or conclusion-less essays lose Task Achievement marks

    07 · IELTS Listening Strategies¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I read the questions during the preview time, every section? Predicting the answer type before listening dramatically improves accuracy
    Did I underline keywords in each question? Helps you track where you are as the audio plays
    Did I check the word/number limit for each question set? Exceeding it makes a correct answer count as wrong
    Did I watch for corrections/changes mentioned by speakers? The final version of information is what's tested, not the first
    Did I answer every question, even with a guess? No penalty for incorrect answers — blanks are pure lost points

    08 · IELTS Reading Strategies¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I skim for structure before answering any questions? Saves far more time than reading passage word-by-word upfront
    Did I distinguish "False" from "Not Given" correctly? The single most common source of lost marks in this test
    Did I underline keywords in questions before scanning? Makes scanning targeted instead of re-reading the whole passage
    Did I watch for paraphrased headings/synonyms, not literal word matches? Questions rarely reuse the passage's exact wording
    Did I move on from a stuck question rather than over-spending time? Protects your ability to answer every question at least once

    09 · Giving a 3-5 Minute Structured Talk¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I state a roadmap near the start? Orients the listener for a multi-minute talk
    Did I use a signpost phrase before each new point? Makes structure audible, not just present in your head
    Did each point include a concrete example? Prevents the talk from sounding like a list of abstract claims
    Did my conclusion restate the main points, not just stop? A proper close is expected at this length
    Did I hit close to the 3-5 minute target? Under 3 minutes suggests underdeveloped points; well over 5 suggests poor time control

    10 · Project — Full Mock IELTS Speaking Interview Script¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I time each part against the real format in section 1? Practicing under real time pressure is the point of a mock test
    Did I write only questions in advance, not answers? Scripted answers don't train spontaneous speech
    Did I record the whole thing in one uninterrupted take? Simulates real exam conditions honestly
    Did I self-score against all four official criteria? Generic "that went well" feedback doesn't drive improvement
    Did I identify one specific weakness to target next time? Turns the mock test into a concrete practice plan

    Level 3 · Advanced¶

    01 · Advanced Fluency Techniques¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I paraphrase instead of going silent when stuck for a word? Keeps the listener engaged and avoids a fluency breakdown
    Did I use filled pauses ("let me think") instead of silence? Sounds like natural thinking, not a stall
    Did I patch corrections in place rather than restarting sentences? Preserves rhythm and sounds more native
    Did I use at least one fixed chunk phrase? Chunking speeds up real-time speech production

    02 · IELTS Band 7+ Speaking Strategies¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I use at least one less-common vocabulary item naturally per answer? Directly targets Lexical Resource band descriptors
    Did I use at least one complex structure (conditional, relative clause, passive, cleft)? Directly targets Grammatical Range
    Did my hesitations sound natural rather than frequent/disruptive? Fluency and Coherence rewards occasional, not constant, hesitation
    Did I avoid forcing in vocabulary that didn't fit the sentence? Unnatural vocabulary use can lower scores rather than raise them

    03 · IELTS Band 7+ Writing Strategies¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I vary my cohesive devices instead of repeating "firstly/moreover"? Rewarded specifically under Coherence and Cohesion
    Did I choose precise words over merely "impressive" ones? Precision with control outscores sophistication with errors
    Does each paragraph include at least two different sentence types? Demonstrates Grammatical Range directly
    Did I fully address every part of the task, not just the easiest part? Task Achievement/Response caps the score if any part is skipped

    04 · Advanced Listening for Academic/Professional Contexts¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I recognize structure signals (lists, contrasts, summaries)? Lets you predict and organize information as it arrives
    Did I use a consistent note-taking system (Cornell, abbreviations)? Speeds up capture without sacrificing accuracy
    Did I focus on content words when speech was fast? Preserves meaning even when you can't catch every word
    Did I flag unclear points instead of getting stuck on them? Prevents missing the next section while dwelling on one gap

    05 · Advanced Reading for Academic Texts¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I identify the text's main thesis before reading in detail? Gives you a framework to fit each paragraph's role into
    Did I notice hedging language and what it signals about certainty? Prevents overstating a writer's claims when summarizing
    Did I read topic sentences first on a difficult text? Reveals the argument's skeleton before you invest in close reading
    Did I infer unfamiliar words from context before looking them up? Builds reading speed and vocabulary inference skill together

    06 · Public Speaking Basics¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I project my voice rather than speaking at conversational volume? Ensures the whole room can hear without straining
    Did I pause after key points instead of rushing on? Gives the audience time to absorb information
    Did I spread eye contact across the room, not just one spot? Builds connection with the whole audience
    Did I use a breathing technique to manage nerves before starting? Reduces physical symptoms of anxiety that affect delivery

    07 · Handling Difficult Questions & Impromptu Speaking¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I start speaking within 2-3 seconds using a buying-time phrase? Avoids an awkward silence that looks like you have no answer
    Did I follow Point → Reason → Example → Point? Gives structure to an answer built with zero preparation
    Did I stay calm and factual if the question was hostile? Prevents escalation and preserves credibility
    Did I admit it honestly when I didn't know something? More credible than guessing or bluffing, and buys goodwill

    08 · Cross-Cultural Communication Nuances¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I read the room's directness level before speaking? Avoids sounding either too blunt or too vague for the context
    Did I soften disagreement and requests appropriately? Preserves working relationships without losing your point
    Did I allow silence after questions instead of rushing to fill it? Silence often signals thinking, not confusion or disagreement
    Did I avoid idioms/humor that might not translate? Keeps communication inclusive in mixed-nationality settings

    09 · Advanced Vocabulary for Professional Contexts¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I use a precise verb instead of "go up/down/change"? Signals fluency and tightens sentences
    Did I hedge appropriately when discussing risk or uncertainty? Avoids overclaiming and sounds professionally calibrated
    Did I use natural collocations rather than word-by-word translation? Prevents sentences that are grammatically correct but sound "off"
    Did I upgrade at least one everyday phrase to professional register? Matches the expected tone of workplace communication

    10 · Project — Deliver a 5-Minute Presentation on a Given Topic¶

    Check Why it matters
    Is my topic scoped to one clear message, not a broad survey? Five minutes only supports one well-developed idea
    Does my opening hook earn attention in the first 20 seconds? Audiences decide whether to keep listening almost immediately
    Did I rehearse with a timer at least twice? Timing drift is the most common reason presentations run over or under
    Did I prepare for likely questions using PREP? Prevents the Q&A section from undoing a strong prepared talk

    Level 4 · Master¶

    01 · Executive-Level Communication in English¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I lead with the conclusion, not the build-up? Executives read build-up as burying the point
    Did I limit myself to one risk and one ask per update? More than one of each dilutes what needs attention
    Did I stay calm and brief when pushed back on? Defensive or lengthy responses erode perceived seniority
    Would this update work if I only had 30 seconds? Forces genuine prioritization of content

    02 · Advanced Persuasive Speaking & Debate Techniques¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I establish credibility before making the claim? Ethos makes the audience willing to engage with the logic
    Did I follow Claim → Evidence → Consequence? Gives the argument a clear, memorable shape
    Did I steelman the counterargument before rebutting it? Makes your rebuttal far more persuasive than dismissing it
    Did I stay calm and non-hostile during pushback? Hostility undermines credibility even when the logic is sound

    03 · IELTS Band 8+ Mastery¶

    Check Why it matters
    Do my speaking hesitations happen only when choosing precise words, not basic ones? This is the core fluency marker distinguishing Band 7 from Band 8
    Does my Task 2 essay use varied cohesive devices, not a mechanical list? Mechanical linking caps cohesion scores below Band 8
    Can I reliably tell False from Not Given in Reading? This single distinction accounts for a large share of lost Reading marks at this level
    Am I self-correcting naturally rather than freezing when I make an error? Natural self-correction is itself a Band 8 fluency marker

    04 · Advanced Academic Writing¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I hedge claims to match the actual strength of the evidence? Overclaiming is one of the most common academic writing errors
    Did I include analysis after evidence, not just evidence alone? Unexplained evidence reads as unfinished argument
    Did I vary how I introduce cited sources (argues/claims/notes)? Repetitive citation verbs read as unpolished
    Does each paragraph have one clear topic sentence? Multi-topic paragraphs weaken argument structure

    05 · Coaching Others in English Fluency¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I diagnose the real bottleneck, not just the surface symptom? Practice aimed at the wrong problem wastes the learner's effort
    Did my feedback name a specific, repeatable pattern? Vague feedback doesn't change behavior
    Did I choose the right correction technique for the moment (recast vs. direct vs. delayed)? Wrong-moment correction breaks confidence or fluency unnecessarily
    Did I end the session on a genuine, specific strength? Ends the session with motivation intact

    06 · Advanced Negotiation Language¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I anchor with a range rather than a single rigid number? A range signals confidence while leaving room to move
    Did I use conditional language when making concessions? Prevents giving away value without getting something back
    Did I push back calmly rather than aggressively? Preserves the relationship for future negotiations
    Did I close with an explicit confirmation of terms? Prevents misunderstandings after the conversation ends

    07 · Media & Interview Skills¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I prepare 3 key messages before the interview? Gives you something solid to bridge back to under pressure
    Did I answer in complete, quotable sentences? Reduces the risk of being misquoted or sounding evasive
    Did I bridge rather than dodge unwanted questions? Bridging looks confident; dodging looks evasive
    Did I resist filling silence after my answer with extra unplanned detail? Extra unplanned detail is often where interview mistakes happen

    08 · Building Long-Term Fluency Habits¶

    Check Why it matters
    Am I hitting the weekly minimums for all four skills? Uneven practice causes uneven skill decay
    Is my habit attached to an existing routine? Standalone "find time" goals are the most commonly abandoned
    Do I have a feedback loop (recordings, a partner, periodic review)? Without feedback, plateaus go unnoticed for months
    Am I deliberately varying register and accent in my input? Prevents blind spots that only surface in real high-stakes situations

    09 · Career Growth: Becoming an English Fluency Coach¶

    Check Why it matters
    Have I identified which specific learner need I'm serving? Determines pricing, format, and marketing language
    Do I have credible evidence of my own ability (certificate, results, portfolio)? Builds trust, especially with strangers online
    Is my offer described in outcome terms, not just "practice"? Outcome framing converts better than vague service descriptions
    Is my marketing language specific rather than superlative? Specificity reads as credible; superlatives read as generic

    10 · Capstone — Full IELTS Mock Test Self-Administration & Band Score Self-Assessment¶

    Check Why it matters
    Did I time every section exactly as the real exam does? Untimed practice doesn't reveal your real exam-day performance
    Did I record my Speaking section for review? Self-assessing speaking from memory is unreliable
    Did I self-assess against band descriptors, not just a gut feeling? Descriptor-based review reveals specific, fixable gaps
    Did I write down one specific fix per section, not just a score? A score alone doesn't tell you what to practice next
