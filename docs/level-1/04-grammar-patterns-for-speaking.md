# 04 · Grammar Patterns for Speaking

You don't need every grammar rule in English to speak fluently — spoken
English leans heavily on a small set of high-frequency structures used
over and over. This module gives you that core set, so you can build
sentences quickly under real-time pressure instead of mentally running
through a full grammar textbook mid-conversation.

## 1. The core tenses that carry most spoken English

| Tense | Form | Used for | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present simple | subject + verb (+s) | Habits, facts, routines | "I work in marketing." |
| Present continuous | am/is/are + verb-ing | Right now, temporary situations | "I'm working on a new project this month." |
| Past simple | verb + -ed (or irregular) | Finished actions at a specific time | "I finished the report yesterday." |
| Present perfect | have/has + past participle | Past action connected to now, no specific time | "I've never been to Japan." |
| Going to future | am/is/are + going to + verb | Plans already decided | "I'm going to call her tomorrow." |
| Will future | will + verb | Decisions made right now, predictions | "I think it'll rain later." |

A common speaking error is overusing present simple for everything
("Yesterday I go to store") because it's the first tense most learners
memorize. Deliberately practicing past simple and present perfect in
speech (not just in writing) fixes this faster than more grammar study.

## 2. Present perfect vs. past simple — the confusion that trips up most learners

| | Past simple | Present perfect |
|---|---|---|
| Time given? | Yes ("yesterday," "in 2019," "last week") | No specific time |
| Connection to now? | No — it's closed, finished | Yes — relevant to the present |
| Example | "I saw that movie last week." | "I've seen that movie." (at some point, unspecified) |
| Example | "She lived in Paris for two years (and doesn't anymore)." | "She has lived in Paris for two years (and still does)." |

**Quick test:** if you can attach a specific past time word to the
sentence naturally, use past simple. If the sentence would sound odd
with a specific time ("I've seen that movie yesterday" ✗), use present
perfect.

## 3. High-frequency sentence patterns for speaking

Memorizing these frames as reusable templates lets you produce fluent
sentences fast, by filling in the blank rather than building from
scratch each time.

| Pattern | Example |
|---|---|
| "I used to ___" (past habit, no longer true) | "I used to live in a small town." |
| "I'm supposed to ___" (obligation/expectation) | "I'm supposed to finish this by Friday." |
| "It depends on ___" | "It depends on how much time I have." |
| "The more ___, the more ___" | "The more I practice, the more confident I feel." |
| "I wish I could ___" | "I wish I could speak French fluently." |
| "What if ___?" | "What if we tried a different approach?" |
| "As long as ___" | "As long as you finish it on time, it's fine." |
| "By the time ___, ___" | "By the time I got there, the meeting had started." |

## 4. Modal verbs for everyday speaking

| Modal | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| can / can't | ability, permission | "I can help you with that." |
| could | past ability, polite request | "Could you repeat that, please?" |
| should | advice, recommendation | "You should try that new restaurant." |
| might / may | possibility | "It might rain later." |
| must | strong obligation, logical conclusion | "You must be exhausted." |
| would | hypothetical, polite offer | "I'd love a coffee, thanks." |

## 5. A worked example — building a fluent answer from patterns

**Question:** "Have you ever changed your mind about something
important?"

**Using the patterns above:**
> "Yeah, actually — I used to think working long hours was the only way
> to get ahead. I've always been someone who says yes to everything, but
> a couple of years ago I burned out pretty badly, and that changed how
> I see it. Now I'd say it depends on the situation — sometimes pushing
> hard is worth it, but the more I think about it, the more I believe
> balance matters just as much as effort. I wish I'd figured that out
> earlier, honestly, but I'm glad I did eventually."

Five different patterns from this module ("used to," "have always been,"
"it depends on," "the more...the more," "I wish I'd") are doing the
structural work, so the speaker can focus on content, not on building
grammar from zero.

## Exercise

Write four sentences about yourself, each one using a different pattern
from section 3 ("I used to," "it depends on," "the more...the more," "I
wish I could"). Then say them out loud, and immediately extend each one
with one more sentence of natural follow-up, the way the worked example
does — don't stop at the pattern, keep talking for at least 15 more
seconds per sentence.
