# 10 · Project — Record and Self-Assess a 2-Minute Short Talk

This project pulls together everything from Level 1: fluency habits
(Module 1), pronunciation (Module 2), vocabulary (Module 3), grammar
patterns (Module 4), listening skills applied to your own speech
(Module 5), talk structure (Module 6), and conversational phrasing
(Module 7) — plus a first taste of the IELTS Speaking format (Modules
8-9). You'll record a real 2-minute talk and score yourself against a
simplified version of the actual IELTS Speaking band descriptors.

## 1. Choose your topic

Pick one of these (or use the one you recorded in Module 1 or Module 6,
if you'd like to compare directly):

- "Describe a person who has influenced you."
- "Describe a memorable trip you took."
- "Describe a goal you're currently working toward."
- "Describe a change you'd like to make in your life."

These are written in the exact style of real IELTS Speaking Part 2 cue
cards, which you'll work with directly in Level 2.

## 2. Plan (60 seconds, timed)

Use the Quick Talk Planner from Module 6:

```text
Topic: ____________________
Main point (one sentence): ____________________
Body point 1 + example: ____________________
Body point 2 + example: ____________________
Closing line: ____________________
```

Give yourself exactly 60 seconds for this — no more. Real exam
conditions (and real conversations) don't give you unlimited prep time,
and the goal of this project is to test your fluency under realistic
pressure, not your ability to write a polished paragraph.

## 3. Record

Record yourself speaking for **1.5-2 minutes**, without stopping,
pausing the recording, or restarting. If you freeze, use a filler
phrase from Module 1 ("let me think about that for a second...") and
keep going rather than stopping the recording.

## 4. Self-assessment rubric

IELTS Speaking is scored on four criteria. Below is a simplified,
plain-language self-assessment version of each — score yourself 1
(needs work), 2 (okay), or 3 (good) on each, honestly.

| Criterion | What to check | Your score (1-3) |
|---|---|---|
| **Fluency & Coherence** | Did you speak continuously, with few long pauses, and did your ideas connect logically (using linking words from Module 6)? | |
| **Lexical Resource** | Did you use varied vocabulary — word families and collocations from Module 3 — rather than repeating the same 3-4 words? | |
| **Grammatical Range & Accuracy** | Did you use more than one tense/pattern from Module 4 (not just present simple for everything)? | |
| **Pronunciation** | Was your word and sentence stress (Module 2) generally correct, and could a listener follow you without replaying? | |

A realistic self-assessment target after completing Level 1 honestly is
scoring 2/3 on most criteria, with some 3s — a 1 anywhere tells you
exactly which earlier module (2, 3, or 4) to revisit before Level 2.

## 5. Structural checklist

Separately from the band-descriptor scoring above, check the talk's
structure against Module 6:

- [ ] Clear intro stating the topic and main point (10-15 seconds)
- [ ] At least two distinct body points, each with a concrete example
- [ ] At least two linking words used naturally (Module 6, section 3)
- [ ] A clean conclusion that restates the main point
- [ ] Total length between 1.5 and 2 minutes

## 6. Compare against Module 1

If you also did the Module 1 exercise (60 seconds on "a typical day in
your life," unplanned), listen to both recordings back to back. You
should notice a real difference even without more overall study time —
fewer long silent gaps, fewer restarted sentences, and a clearer sense
of where the talk is going at each point. That difference *is* what
Level 1 was training — write down, in 2-3 sentences, what specifically
sounds different between the two recordings.

## Exercise

Complete the full project above: plan (60 seconds), record (1.5-2
minutes), score yourself on the rubric in section 4, check the
structural checklist in section 5, and write your 2-3 sentence
comparison against your Module 1 recording. Save both the recording and
your self-assessment notes — Level 2's Module 10 project (a full mock
IELTS Speaking interview) will ask you to repeat this kind of
self-assessment at a noticeably higher difficulty level, and having this
baseline will make the improvement visible.
