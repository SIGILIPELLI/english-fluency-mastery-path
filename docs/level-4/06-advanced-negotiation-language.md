# 06 · Advanced Negotiation Language

Negotiation in English relies on a specific set of phrases for
anchoring positions, making conditional offers, and closing without
appearing desperate or overly aggressive. This module covers the
language patterns used in salary negotiations, vendor contracts, and
cross-team resourcing discussions.

## 1. Opening and anchoring

| Function | Phrase |
|---|---|
| State an opening position | "Based on market data, I'd expect a range of X to Y." |
| Anchor without seeming rigid | "My starting point would be around X, though I'm open to discussing it." |
| Ask the other side to anchor first | "What did you have in mind for this?" |

## 2. Conditional (if-then) offers

Conditional language lets you make an offer contingent on something,
which keeps you from giving away value for free.

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| "If X, then Y" | "If we can agree on a 12-month term, I can offer a 10% discount." |
| "I could do X, provided Y" | "I could move the deadline up, provided we get sign-off by Monday." |
| "That depends on..." | "That depends on whether the scope stays fixed." |

## 3. Pushing back without damaging the relationship

| Situation | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Rejecting an offer | "I appreciate the offer, but that doesn't quite work for me." |
| Countering | "I hear you — could we meet in the middle at X?" |
| Buying time before responding | "Let me think that over and get back to you tomorrow." |
| Naming a dealbreaker calmly | "I want to be upfront — X is a hard requirement for me." |

## 4. Closing language

| Function | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Confirming agreement | "So, to confirm, we've agreed on X — is that right?" |
| Summarizing next steps | "I'll send over the revised terms by end of day." |
| Leaving the door open if no deal | "Let's leave it here for now and revisit next quarter." |

## 5. Worked example — a short salary negotiation exchange

> **Manager:** "We can offer $85,000 for this role."
>
> **Candidate:** "I appreciate the offer. Based on my research and the
> scope of this role, I was expecting something closer to $95,000.
> Is there flexibility there?"
>
> **Manager:** "We could go to $88,000, but that's close to our ceiling
> for this level."
>
> **Candidate:** "I understand budget constraints. If we could settle
> at $90,000, along with a review at six months instead of twelve,
> I'd be comfortable moving forward."
>
> **Manager:** "I can agree to that."
>
> **Candidate:** "Great — to confirm, $90,000 with a six-month review.
> I'm glad we found something that works for both sides."

This exchange shows anchoring, a conditional counter-offer, and a
clean confirmation close — without hostility on either side.

## 6. Cheat sheet — negotiation language checklist

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Did I anchor with a range rather than a single rigid number? | A range signals confidence while leaving room to move |
| Did I use conditional language when making concessions? | Prevents giving away value without getting something back |
| Did I push back calmly rather than aggressively? | Preserves the relationship for future negotiations |
| Did I close with an explicit confirmation of terms? | Prevents misunderstandings after the conversation ends |

## Exercise

Write out both sides of a negotiation you expect to have soon (salary,
a vendor contract, a deadline extension). Script your opening anchor,
one conditional offer, one calm pushback line, and your closing
confirmation. Practice delivering your side aloud with a partner
playing the other role.
