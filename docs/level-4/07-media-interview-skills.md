# 07 · Media & Interview Skills

Speaking to a journalist, on a podcast, or in a high-stakes job
interview requires a different skill set from ordinary conversation:
staying on message, bridging away from unwanted questions gracefully,
and answering in complete, quotable sentences rather than fragments.
This module covers those techniques.

## 1. The bridging technique

Bridging lets you acknowledge a question — even one you don't want to
answer directly — and pivot to your key message without appearing
evasive.

| Step | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Acknowledge | "That's a fair question." |
| Bridge | "What I think is more important here is..." / "What I'd really emphasize is..." |
| Deliver your message | (your prepared key point) |

> **Example:** "Isn't this launch just a copy of your competitor's
> product?" → "That's a fair question — a lot of companies operate in
> this space. What I'd really emphasize is what makes our approach
> different: [key point]."

## 2. Speaking in quotable, complete sentences

Journalists and interviewers often lift a single sentence as a quote.
Answers built as complete, self-contained sentences are far more
likely to be represented accurately than fragmented, rambling ones.

| Fragmented (risky) | Quotable (safe) |
|---|---|
| "Well, I guess it's kind of about, you know, making things better for users, mostly." | "Our goal is simple: make the product genuinely easier for users, not just look easier." |

## 3. Preparing key messages in advance

Before any interview, prepare 3 core messages you want to land
regardless of the specific questions asked.

| Element | Example |
|---|---|
| Key message 1 | The product solves a specific, named problem |
| Key message 2 | One concrete proof point (a number, a customer story) |
| Key message 3 | A forward-looking statement about what's next |

Whatever is asked, look for a natural bridge back to one of these
three points at least once during the conversation.

## 4. Handling hostile or "gotcha" questions

| Situation | Response pattern |
|---|---|
| Loaded question with a false premise | "I'd actually push back on the premise there — it's not that we ignored X, it's that we prioritized Y first." |
| Asked to speculate | "I don't want to speculate, but what I can tell you is..." |
| Asked something off-limits (legal, confidential) | "That's not something I'm able to comment on at this stage." |
| Silence used as pressure (interviewer waits after your answer) | Resist the urge to fill silence with extra, unplanned detail — a complete answer can stand on its own |

## 5. Worked example — a full interview exchange

> **Interviewer:** "Some critics say this feature is a privacy risk.
> How do you respond to that?"
>
> **Answer:** "That's a concern worth taking seriously, and privacy
> was actually the first thing our design team addressed. What I'd
> emphasize is that all the data processing happens on-device, not on
> our servers — that's a deliberate architectural choice, not a
> talking point. So while I understand the concern in general, I'd
> push back on it applying to this specific feature."

This response acknowledges the concern (not dismissive), bridges to a
concrete proof point, and closes with a calm, specific pushback —
avoiding both defensiveness and over-apologizing.

## 6. Cheat sheet — media & interview checklist

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Did I prepare 3 key messages before the interview? | Gives you something solid to bridge back to under pressure |
| Did I answer in complete, quotable sentences? | Reduces the risk of being misquoted or sounding evasive |
| Did I bridge rather than dodge unwanted questions? | Bridging looks confident; dodging looks evasive |
| Did I resist filling silence after my answer with extra unplanned detail? | Extra unplanned detail is often where interview mistakes happen |

## Exercise

Write your 3 key messages for a real or hypothetical interview about
your work. Have a partner ask you 5 questions, including at least one
hostile or off-topic one, without telling you in advance. Practice
bridging back to your key messages and review the recording for
fragmented versus quotable answers.
