# 03 · IELTS Speaking Part 2

Part 2 is the "long turn": the examiner hands you a task card with a
topic and three or four bullet points, gives you **1 minute to prepare
notes**, and then you must **speak for 1-2 minutes** without
interruption. It's the part that trips up the most candidates, because
it's the only section of the whole IELTS test where you have to sustain
monologue speech rather than answer short questions.

## 1. What the task card looks like

Every Part 2 card follows the same format: a topic sentence, then three
or four "you should say" bullet points, then a final open-ended
question.

> **Describe a skill you learned that you found useful.**
> You should say:
> - what the skill is
> - how you learned it
> - how long it took you to learn
> and explain why you found this skill useful.

The bullet points aren't optional extras — they're your built-in
structure. Covering all of them (plus the final question) is usually
enough to fill the full 2 minutes without running out of things to say.

## 2. Using the 1-minute preparation time

You get a pencil and paper and exactly 60 seconds. The biggest mistake
is trying to write full sentences — there's no time, and you'll end up
reading instead of speaking naturally.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Write single words or short phrases per bullet | Write full sentences |
| Jot a rough timeline/order to speak in | Try to script the whole answer word-for-word |
| Note 1-2 specific details (names, numbers, places) | Leave bullets blank because you can't think of a "true" answer — invent one |
| Note a linking word for each bullet ("first," "then," "the reason was") | Worry about grammar in your notes — nobody sees them |

**It is completely acceptable to invent or slightly exaggerate your
answer.** The examiner is scoring your English, not fact-checking your
biography.

## 3. A simple structure that fills 2 minutes

| Time | Content |
|---|---|
| ~15s | Introduce the topic directly ("I'd like to talk about...") |
| ~60-70s | Work through each bullet point in order, with one specific detail per point |
| ~20-30s | Answer the final question / add a reflection or consequence |
| ~10s | A closing sentence, so you don't trail off mid-thought |

Running short (under 1 minute) is the single most common Part 2 failure
— it strongly signals to the examiner that you struggle to sustain
speech, which hurts Fluency and Coherence.

## 4. Worked example — full 2-minute answer

> **Card:** Describe a skill you learned that you found useful.

> I'd like to talk about learning to touch-type, which is something I
> picked up a couple of years ago. Before that I used to type with two
> fingers, looking at the keyboard the whole time, which was incredibly
> slow whenever I had to write reports for work.
>
> I actually learned it using a free typing website that gives you
> daily lessons — you start with just the home row of keys, and it
> gradually builds up until you're typing full sentences without
> looking down at all. I practiced for about fifteen minutes every
> morning before work, which doesn't sound like much, but it adds up
> quickly.
>
> It took me around six weeks before I really noticed a difference, and
> maybe three months before it felt completely natural — to the point
> where I don't even think about where the keys are anymore.
>
> As for why I found it useful — well, the obvious reason is speed, but
> what I didn't expect was how much it changed the way I write. Because
> I'm not distracted by hunting for keys, I can actually focus on what
> I'm trying to say, so my emails and reports come out clearer and
> faster. Looking back, it's probably one of the most practical things
> I've learned as an adult, even though it seemed like a small,
> unglamorous skill at the time.

This answer covers all three bullets in order, adds specific numbers
(fifteen minutes, six weeks, three months), and closes with a
reflection rather than trailing off — filling close to the full two
minutes naturally.

## 5. Cheat sheet — Part 2 checklist

| Check | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Did I use all 60 seconds to plan, in short phrases only? | Full sentences waste time and lead to reading, not speaking |
| Did I address every bullet point on the card? | Directly supports Coherence — shows a complete, organized answer |
| Did I speak for close to the full 1-2 minutes? | Running under a minute is read as a fluency weakness |
| Did I add at least one specific, concrete detail? | Vague answers score lower on Lexical Resource |
| Did I finish with a proper closing sentence? | Avoids sounding like you simply ran out of things to say |

## Exercise

Pick three Part 2 topics (e.g. "Describe a place you'd like to visit,"
"Describe a person who has influenced you," "Describe a piece of
technology you find useful"). For each: time yourself doing exactly 60
seconds of note-taking in short phrases, then record yourself speaking
for 1-2 minutes using the structure in section 3. Play each recording
back and check it against the section 5 checklist, especially total
speaking time.
